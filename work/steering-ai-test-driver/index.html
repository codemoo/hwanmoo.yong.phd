AI test driver for next-generation steering systems

Research·2023–24·with an auto parts maker · technical lead

  • ~40%
    lower peak lateral acceleration than the standard driver at the same slalom pace

A new steering system is judged in simulation before it is ever fitted to a real car, and the verdict depends on who is driving. Limit maneuvers such as a slalom or an emergency lane change must be driven precisely, the same way every run. In those maneuvers the simulator's standard driver swayed the car too much, so a bad run could be the steering's fault or the driver's.

Commercial vehicle simulators are not built for AI training, so I first turned one into a training environment. Learning needs countless test drives, so the environment runs simulator copies in parallel across machines, each trading data live with the training code. Every driver learns there by trial and error and is scored on a suite of limit-maneuver tests.

In this environment we developed two kinds of AI driver. In one the AI does the steering itself; in the other, a path-following controller drives while the AI retunes the controller's settings moment by moment. We also tested pretraining on existing controllers' driving data, so a driver need not start from scratch when the setup changes.

The AI drivers handle limit maneuvers steadily in simulation and were delivered to the client with the training environment. On the same slalom at the same pace as the standard driver, the direct-steering driver kept the car much steadier. Its peak lateral acceleration was nearly 40% lower.
