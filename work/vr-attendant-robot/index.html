Customer-aware AI attendant robot for VR simulators

Research·2017–18·Government R&D

Labor is a major cost in a VR simulator venue, where every machine needs staff to seat riders, check they are ready and start each game. We built an AI robot that takes over this work, speaks to each customer personally and even gives live commentary on their drive. The game was a commercial title with no interface for training, so in a live venue the robot had to read every situation in real time from the game's driving data alone.

From its cameras, the robot estimates each rider's gender and age group, and once the rider sits down and grips the wheel, it starts the game on its own. Convolutional neural networks trained on public face datasets misjudged local customers' gender and age, so we retrained them on a dataset of local faces. The headset hides the upper face, so I developed a way to estimate emotion from the lower face alone, and that work led to a patent on simulator control.

During the drive, the robot reads the game's live data and calls chases, overtakes and collisions in a voice and tone chosen for that rider. I was the main developer of the venue software: its servers and data pipeline, the commentary logic, and the robot and remote-operator links. Real-time decisions run on an on-site server so the commentary keeps pace with the drive, while the cloud stores video and game data and retrains the models.

In a field test at a live VR venue with hundreds of visitors, those who heard the robot were significantly more satisfied. The study was published in IEEE Transactions on Automation Science and Engineering, with me as co-first author. Two granted patents followed, both with me as co-inventor: "Concierge robot and method of providing personalized service using the same" and "VR simulator control method using emotional state estimation."
