An international freight forwarder ran its operations on email, trading hundreds of messages a day in multiple languages with customers, carriers and other partners. Staff read every one, worked out what it meant for the order in question and replied by hand. Nearly half of what they sent was a standard template reply.

I designed and built the whole LLM pipeline, from mail intake to a side panel beside the staff's existing in-house system. It pieces each conversation back together, picks out every request and sorts it by task in light of earlier emails. The same email always gets the same answer, and a request the system cannot back with evidence goes to staff instead of getting a made-up answer.

Every model runs on servers dedicated to the client, so no email ever goes to a cloud AI service. A language model reads the mail, and models I trained on the client's own mail make the decisions. Accuracy is checked against past orders the system has never seen.

In live use, the system read and sorted about 18,000 of the client's emails by task, with labels typically on screen a minute or so after an email arrived. An analysis of one week of real orders estimated a 30% cut in outgoing email work, or up to 47% once two complex order flows are standardized. At 100,000 emails a month, mail analysis was modeled at one-sixth to one-ninth the cost of cloud AI.
